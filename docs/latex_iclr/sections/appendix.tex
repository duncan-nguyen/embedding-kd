\section{Training Details}
\label{app:training-hyperparameters}

\paragraph{Baseline implementation policy.}
We use an author-released public implementation whenever one is available. For
methods without public code, we reimplement the objective and training
procedure described in the corresponding paper. All implementations are
integrated into the same training and evaluation framework so that the
distillation corpus, teacher--student checkpoints, batch construction, and
downstream evaluators are shared. We retain method-specific components, such as
Stella's two-stage schedule and TALAS's adaptive SAM optimizer, rather than
replacing them with GATE-KD's recipe.

The retained objective settings are as follows. RKD uses distance and angle
weights 25 and 50, with its in-batch task term weighted 0.1; this task weight is
the one deliberate departure from the published value of 1 under our five-epoch
budget. Stella retains its $2+3$-epoch two-stage schedule and stage weights
$(0.5,2.0,0.5)$ for cosine, similarity, and triplet terms. CDM uses
$\alpha_{\mathrm{DTW}}=0.5$ and unit CLS weight; DSKD uses
$\alpha_{\mathrm{DTW}}=1$ with bidirectional cross-attention; EMO uses two
student layers, unit attention and optimal-transport weights, and
$\alpha_{\mathrm{OT}}=0.1$. TALAS retains its final-two-layer structural term,
teacher anchor, adaptive SAM with $\rho=0.05$, and weights
$(w_{\mathrm{task}},w_{\mathrm{KD}},w_{\mathrm{struct}})=(0.001,0.75,1)$.
GATE-KD uses only its endpoint and $H_0$ terms in the main comparison. These
choices preserve each baseline's defining mechanism, but the common budget
below changes published batch sizes or durations where they differ. We
therefore evaluate controlled implementations under one budget, not the best
performance attainable after method- and model-pair-specific tuning.

All main-comparison runs use a maximum sequence length of 256 and seeds 42, 43,
and 44. We fix the batch size at 128 and the total training duration at five
epochs for every method; Stella's two stages comprise two and three epochs.
Learning rate is the only optimization hyperparameter selected separately for
each method. Selection is performed on validation data before the three-seed
test sweep; the selected rate is then held fixed across all three
teacher--student configurations and seeds, and the final-epoch checkpoint is
evaluated once on the test splits, so no test score enters learning-rate or
checkpoint selection. Every method used the common grid
$\{1,2,3,5,7\}\times10^{-5}$ and no other per-method tuning.
All methods use AdamW with weight decay $0.01$ and a cosine schedule ending at
$2\times10^{-6}$. TALAS wraps AdamW in adaptive SAM.
For GATE-KD,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{topo}}=0.5$, the $H_0$
cloud is the current training batch, the gauge is refitted after each non-final
epoch, and gauge fitting uses the full distillation corpus. These settings are
fixed before test evaluation; the sensitivity sweeps are diagnostic rather than
part of model selection. Table~\ref{tab:training-hyperparameters} lists the
selected learning rates, optimizers, and warm-up ratios.

\input{tables/training_hyperparameters}

\paragraph{Optimization-timing scope.}
CUDA events measure each minibatch update, including the student forward pass,
loss computation, backward pass, and optimizer update. Mean update time and
throughput exclude the first ten warm-up steps; cumulative optimization time
sums all measured updates across five epochs. One-time preparation,
epoch-boundary gauge refitting, model and data loading, and final evaluation
fall outside this timed region. The archived sweep includes co-located
processes, so the reported values characterize the measured optimization
environment rather than isolated-process or end-to-end efficiency.

\input{tables/training_efficiency}

Within this boundary, GATE-KD records the lowest mean update time and
cumulative optimization time and the highest throughput in all three
configurations. Two GATE-KD-specific costs fall outside it. The PCA fit is one
SVD of the 14{,}760-row cache, and the teacher cache itself is shared with
TALAS, RKD, and Stella. Each of the four epoch-boundary refits re-encodes the
gauge-fit corpus with the student; charging those forward passes at the
training-step throughput of Table~\ref{tab:training-efficiency}, an upper bound
because it does not subtract the backward pass, adds at most 0.64, 0.43, and
0.49 minutes in configurations (a), (b), and (c). We report this accounting to
bound a method-specific omission, not as a directly measured end-to-end time.
Teacher caching, PCA, refit I/O, model loading, evaluation, and
baseline-specific preprocessing are not jointly timed; consequently we make no
claim that Table~\ref{tab:training-efficiency} establishes lower end-to-end
training cost.

\section{Endpoint-Interface Analysis}
\label{app:endpoint-analysis}

\subsection{Robustness to Fixed-Subspace Construction}
\label{app:pca-controls}

We isolate teacher projection in configuration (c), holding the full GATE-KD
objective (native-space $H_0$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{topo}}=0.5$), student,
optimizer, schedule, and epoch-wise Procrustes fixed. These matched reruns need
not equal the main comparison numerically. The reference fits PCA directions on
the centered cache but applies them without subtracting the mean;
mean-subtracted PCA applies this affine centering, and whitening also rescales
by inverse square root eigenvalues. Random controls use three independent Haar
subspaces of the same rank. Each row averages seeds 42--44, with random draws
kept separate.

\input{tables/pca_preprocessing}

Mean subtraction lowers Avg. from 74.79 to 72.09 ($-2.70$), and whitening to
71.59 ($-3.20$), with both source and non-source averages declining. Aligned random subspaces reach
74.45--74.66, only 0.13--0.34 below PCA despite retaining 0.374--0.381 rather
than 0.928 of the teacher energy. The updated uncentered-SVD run reaches 74.60,
0.19 points below PCA overall. Preserving the mean component at application
time therefore matters, whereas once the gauge is aligned neither retained
energy nor the exact fitted subspace is decisive; PCA is numerically best, but
not established as uniquely optimal.

Retained energy and the endpoint objective measure different quantities. The
former is raw coordinate reconstruction before row normalization, whereas the
latter acts on normalized directions after projection and alignment. A random
subspace can therefore discard substantial total variance without necessarily
discarding angular distinctions useful to the student. Concentration in a
high-dimensional projection and redundancy in the teacher representation are
plausible explanations, but we did not measure normalized Gram distortion for
the random projections. The result consequently narrows our claim: PCA is a
stable deterministic default, while the evidence attributes more importance to
student-conditioned orientation than to maximum retained energy.

\subsection{Endpoint-Interface Rank at Fixed Student Width}
\label{app:target-rank}

We isolate the subspace selected by the endpoint interface in configuration
(c). The student architecture, 384-dimensional output, distillation corpus,
optimizer, training schedule, and Procrustes updates remain fixed, while the
retained PCA rank varies over $k\in\{64,128,256,384\}$. This experiment uses
the endpoint-only objective. Targets with $k<384$ are zero-padded to the
student width before Procrustes alignment, which preserves their Gram matrix
and active rank.

Retained energy is measured on the complete teacher cache. On a fixed sample
of 2{,}048 normalized teacher embeddings $T$, we measure the normalized
squared Gram distortion
$\lVert TT^\top-Y_kY_k^\top\rVert_F^2/\lVert TT^\top\rVert_F^2$, where $Y_k$
is the normalized rank-$k$ PCA target. We also report the normalized
Eckart--Young rank floor
$\sum_{j>k}\sigma_j(T)^4/\sum_j\sigma_j(T)^4$
\citep{eckart1936approximation}. The Gram quantities are independent of
the orthogonal gauge and are therefore computed once, while downstream scores
are averaged over three seeds.

Figure~\ref{fig:target-rank} shows monotonic changes in all three quantities.
Increasing $k$ from 64 to 384 raises retained teacher energy from 59.32\% to
92.84\%, reduces normalized Gram distortion from 6.797\% to 0.086\%, and
improves Avg. from 72.57 to 74.27. The intermediate ranks 128 and 256 attain
73.77 and 74.21 Avg., respectively. Most of the downstream benefit is therefore
recovered by rank 256: it captures 96\% of the 64-to-384 improvement, with the
final increase to rank 384 contributing only 0.06 points in this configuration.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/width_bottleneck}
    \caption{Sensitivity to retained PCA rank at fixed student width in
    configuration (c), endpoint-only objective, one corpus, four ranks.
    Targets with $k<384$ are zero-padded before Procrustes alignment. Downstream
    scores show mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over three seeds; retained
    energy and Gram distortion are gauge-independent and computed once.}
    \label{fig:target-rank}
\end{figure}

PCA distortion remains roughly 21--25 times above the rank floor because its
objective differs from normalized Gram approximation: PCA minimizes
reconstruction error of the raw coordinates, whereas the Gram quantity
compares inner products after re-normalizing each projected row, and
re-normalization amplifies the truncated tail for rows with small retained
norm. The floor therefore bounds irreducible error rather than predicting this
interface's distortion, and retained energy should not be read as a proxy for
how well the teacher's angular geometry is preserved; only the Gram distortion
column measures that directly. Two further scope notes apply. First, because
the padded target has zero coordinates outside its rotated $k$-dimensional
subspace, the cosine endpoint loss drives the student's remaining $384-k$
output coordinates toward zero rather than leaving them free, so for $k<384$
the student effectively operates at rank $k$ despite its 384-dimensional
output. Second, the sweep is endpoint-only, in configuration (c), on one
corpus; we have not repeated it with the $H_0$ term or in configurations (a)
and (b).

\subsection{Structural-Control Definitions}
\label{app:structural-control-definitions}

Table~\ref{tab:structural-controls} compares the optional native-space $H_0$
term with structural alternatives under the same endpoint interface and
optimization protocol. The ``None'' row repeats the endpoint-only reference;
the $H_0$ row repeats configuration (c) from the main comparison.

\input{tables/structural_controls}

The sorted spanning-path control constructs a geometry-independent path through
the shuffled batch and matches the independently sorted student and teacher
edge lengths. The kNN control similarly matches independently sorted directed
nearest-neighbor distances with $k=1$. Teacher-MST first selects the teacher's
$B-1$ minimum-spanning-tree edges and then compares student distances on those
same sample pairs, retaining edge correspondence. Native Gram matching compares
all pairwise cosine similarities. Finally, $H_0$ matches independently sorted
student and teacher MST edge weights and discards edge correspondence. The
path, Teacher-MST, and $H_0$ controls expose $B-1$ scalar constraints per batch,
kNN exposes $B$, and Gram matching is deliberately denser.

Among the evaluated controls and weights, $H_0$ produces the largest positive
change. The comparison rules out several simple alternatives but does not
isolate MST sorting or persistence-specific information: the objectives also
differ in graph construction, correspondence, density, and gradient behavior.

\subsection{Implementation of the \texorpdfstring{$H_0$}{H0} Loss}
\label{app:h0-implementation}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:h0-loss} makes the nonsmooth implementation explicit. The
teacher branch is evaluated without gradients. On the student branch, the MST
edge selection and sorting permutation are detached, while the selected edge
weights remain differentiable. Exact distance ties are passed to SciPy in
row-major upper-triangular order. Although the selected active tree can depend
on this library tie-break, every valid MST has the same sorted multiset of edge
weights; the backward pass follows the particular active tree returned in that
forward pass. All reported runs use chord distance, FP32 Gram computation,
$\varepsilon=10^{-7}$ inside the square root, one 128-example cloud per full
training batch (and the complete smaller tail batch), and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{topo}}=0.5$ for the combined objective.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Native-space $H_0$ connectivity-scale loss for one minibatch}
\label{alg:h0-loss}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Row-normalized teacher and student batches $T_{\mathcal B}$ and
$Z_{\mathcal B}$; numerical floor $\varepsilon$.
\For{$A\in\{T_{\mathcal B},Z_{\mathcal B}\}$}
    \State Compute $G\gets AA^\top$ in FP32 and symmetrize
    $G\gets(G+G^\top)/2$.
    \State Compute $D_{ij}\gets\sqrt{\max(2-2G_{ij},\varepsilon)}$ and set
    $D_{ii}\gets0$.
    \State On a detached CPU float64 copy, add the same positive constant to
    every off-diagonal edge and select an undirected MST with SciPy from the
    upper triangle.
    \State Gather the selected weights from the unshifted $D$ and sort them to
    obtain $q^A_{(1:B-1)}$.
\EndFor
\State \Return $(B-1)^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}(q^Z_{(j)}-q^T_{(j)})^2$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Exploratory Representation Diagnostics}
\label{app:layerwise-cka}

We examine whether final-embedding supervision changes the internal
representations of the student even though GATE-KD applies no
intermediate-layer objective. The probe contains 512 unique sentences sampled
from the validation splits after excluding sentences found in the distillation
corpus. For every teacher and student Transformer layer, we compute centered
linear CKA between their pooled representations \citep{kornblith2019cka}.
Results for the trained students are averaged over seeds 42, 43, and 44. We
omit the embedding-output layer because special-token pooling makes this
representation constant across examples, for which centered CKA is undefined.

Figure~\ref{fig:layerwise-raw-cka} shows that the pretrained students already
share substantial representation structure with their teachers. Raw CKA alone
therefore combines correspondence inherited from pretraining with changes
induced by distillation.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/layerwise_raw_cka_all_pairs}
    \caption{Layerwise linear CKA for the pretrained student, GATE-KD, and
    TALAS across three configurations. Trained results are averaged over three
    seeds.}
    \label{fig:layerwise-raw-cka}
\end{figure}

To contextualize representation similarity relative to initialization,
Figure~\ref{fig:layerwise-delta-cka} subtracts the pretrained-student CKA matrix
from the mean matrix after distillation. Final-to-final CKA is higher for
GATE-KD than for TALAS in every configuration: 0.8266, 0.8447, and 0.7845
versus 0.7741, 0.7901, and 0.7333 in configurations (a), (b), and (c), a
difference of 0.051 to 0.055 that is also present in the change from
initialization.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/layerwise_delta_cka_all_pairs}
    \caption{Change in layerwise linear CKA relative to the pretrained
    student. Red and blue denote increased and decreased similarity,
    respectively.}
    \label{fig:layerwise-delta-cka}
\end{figure}

The changes spread over several later student layers rather than one
teacher--student pair, and some layer pairs decrease, so final-embedding
supervision co-occurs with changes in later layers without uniformly stronger
alignment at every depth. CKA is descriptive: it does not establish that the observed
changes cause downstream improvements or that endpoint supervision
mechanistically substitutes for intermediate-layer distillation. It is also
invariant to orthogonal transformations and isotropic scaling, so higher CKA
measures representational similarity rather than transferred task
information; in particular it is blind to the orthogonal gauge itself, so
nothing in these figures bears on the coordinate-alignment claims of
Section~\ref{sec:analysis-gauge}. The figures report seed means only; we have not measured
seed-level variance of the CKA matrices, included a fixed-PCA control without
a fitted gauge, or tested whether per-layer CKA changes correlate with
downstream differences. We therefore use these probes to document that
endpoint supervision co-occurs with changes in later layers, and for nothing
stronger.
