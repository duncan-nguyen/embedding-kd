\section{Proofs of Supporting Results}
\label{app:supporting-proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge-optimality}]
For unit-norm rows,
$1-\langle\mathbf{z}_i^{(e)},\mathbf{y}_iR\rangle
=\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\mathbf{z}_i^{(e)}-\mathbf{y}_iR\rVert_2^2$.
Summing over $\mathcal{C}$ gives Equation~\ref{eq:endpoint-procrustes}.
Expanding the Frobenius norm shows that minimizing it over $R\in O(d_S)$ is
equivalent to maximizing
$\operatorname{tr}(R^\top Y_{\mathcal{C}}^\top Z_{\mathcal{C}}^{(e)})$.
If $Y_{\mathcal{C}}^\top Z_{\mathcal{C}}^{(e)}=U\Sigma V^\top$, the orthogonal
Procrustes solution is $R=UV^\top$, proving optimality. Finally,
$(YR)(YR)^\top=YRR^\top Y^\top=YY^\top$ for every orthogonal $R$. The same
global transformation is applied rowwise, so sentence--target correspondence
is also unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:gram-lower-bound}]
The eigenvalues of $TT^\top$ are $\sigma_j(T)^2$, whereas
$\operatorname{rank}(ZZ^\top)\leq d_S$. The Eckart--Young theorem therefore
lower-bounds the squared error of any such rank-$d_S$ approximation by the
squared tail eigenvalues \citep{eckart1936approximation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:h0-transport-stability}]
Optimal transport on the real line pairs equally weighted samples in sorted
order, which gives Equation~\ref{eq:topological-wasserstein}
\citep{peyre2019computational}. Under a uniform edge perturbation of at most
$\epsilon$, every threshold graph at scale $a$ in either metric is contained
in the other at scale $a+\epsilon$. Kruskal's characterization of the ordered
minimum-spanning-tree edges then gives
$|q^S_{(j)}-q^T_{(j)}|\leq\epsilon$ for every $j$, and averaging their
squared differences proves the bound. This is the degree-zero finite-space
case of Vietoris--Rips persistence stability \citep{chazal2014persistence}.
\end{proof}

\section{Training Details}
\label{app:training-hyperparameters}

All main-comparison runs use a maximum sequence length of 256 and seeds 42, 43,
and 44. We fix the batch size at 128 and the total training duration at five
epochs for every method; Stella's two stages comprise two and three epochs.
Learning rate is the only optimization hyperparameter selected separately for
each method. Selection is performed on validation data before the three-seed
test sweep; the selected rate is then held fixed across all three
teacher--student configurations and seeds. The final-epoch checkpoint is
evaluated once on the test splits. No test score is used for learning-rate or
checkpoint selection. Table~\ref{tab:training-hyperparameters} reports every
selected rate and the method-specific optimizer settings. For every method,
the learning rate was tuned over the common grid
$\{1,2,3,5,7\}\times10^{-5}$; no other optimizer hyperparameter was tuned
per method.
All methods use AdamW with weight decay $0.01$ and a cosine schedule ending at
$2\times10^{-6}$. TALAS wraps AdamW in adaptive SAM.
For GATE-KD,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{topo}}=0.5$, the $H_0$
cloud is the current training batch, the gauge is refitted after each non-final
epoch, and gauge fitting uses the full distillation corpus. These settings are
fixed before test evaluation; the sensitivity sweeps are diagnostic rather than
part of model selection. Table~\ref{tab:training-hyperparameters} lists the
selected learning rates, optimizers, and warm-up ratios.

\paragraph{Optimization-timing scope.}
CUDA events measure each minibatch update, including the student forward pass,
loss computation, backward pass, and optimizer update. Mean update time and
throughput exclude the first ten warm-up steps; cumulative optimization time
sums all measured updates across five epochs. One-time preparation,
epoch-boundary gauge refitting, model and data loading, and final evaluation
fall outside this timed region. The archived sweep includes co-located
processes, so the reported values characterize the measured optimization
environment rather than isolated-process or end-to-end efficiency.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Endpoint-Interface Rank at Fixed Student Width}
\label{app:target-rank}

We isolate the subspace selected by the endpoint interface in configuration
(c). The student architecture, 384-dimensional output, distillation corpus,
optimizer, training schedule, and Procrustes updates remain fixed, while the
retained PCA rank varies over $k\in\{64,128,256,384\}$. This experiment uses
the endpoint-only objective. Targets with $k<384$ are embedded in the native
student space by appending zero coordinates before Procrustes alignment. This
padding preserves their Gram matrix and active rank, and does not change the
capacity or width of the student.

Retained energy is measured on the complete teacher cache. On a fixed sample
of 2{,}048 normalized teacher embeddings $T$, we measure the normalized
squared Gram distortion
$\lVert TT^\top-Y_kY_k^\top\rVert_F^2/\lVert TT^\top\rVert_F^2$, where $Y_k$
is the normalized rank-$k$ PCA target. We also report the normalized rank
floor $\sum_{j>k}\sigma_j(T)^4/\sum_j\sigma_j(T)^4$ from
Equation~\ref{eq:gram-lower-bound}. The Gram quantities are independent of
the orthogonal gauge and are therefore computed once, while downstream scores
are averaged over three seeds.

Figure~\ref{fig:target-rank} shows monotonic changes in all three quantities.
Increasing $k$ from 64 to 384 raises retained teacher energy from 59.32\% to
92.84\%, reduces normalized Gram distortion from 6.797\% to 0.086\%, and
improves Avg. from 72.57 to 74.27. The intermediate ranks 128 and 256 attain
73.77 and 74.21 Avg., respectively. Most of the downstream benefit is therefore
recovered by rank 256: it captures 96\% of the 64-to-384 improvement, with the
final increase to rank 384 contributing only 0.06 points in this configuration.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/width_bottleneck}
    \caption{Sensitivity to retained PCA rank at fixed student width in
    configuration (c), endpoint-only objective, one corpus, four ranks.
    Targets with $k<384$ are zero-padded before Procrustes alignment. Downstream
    scores show mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over three seeds; retained
    energy and Gram distortion are gauge-independent and computed once.}
    \label{fig:target-rank}
\end{figure}

PCA distortion remains roughly 21--25 times above the rank floor because its
objective differs from normalized Gram approximation: PCA minimizes
reconstruction error of the raw coordinates, whereas the Gram quantity
compares inner products after re-normalizing each projected row, and
re-normalization amplifies the truncated tail for rows with small retained
norm. The floor therefore bounds irreducible error rather than predicting this
interface's distortion, and retained energy should not be read as a proxy for
how well the teacher's angular geometry is preserved; only the Gram distortion
column measures that directly. Two further scope notes apply. First, because
the padded target has zero coordinates outside its rotated $k$-dimensional
subspace, the cosine endpoint loss drives the student's remaining $384-k$
output coordinates toward zero rather than leaving them free, so for $k<384$
the student effectively operates at rank $k$ despite its 384-dimensional
output. Second, the sweep is endpoint-only, in configuration (c), on one
corpus; we have not repeated it with the $H_0$ term or in configurations (a)
and (b). Within these limits, increasing rank improves geometry and utility
with diminishing returns. With the student architecture fixed, this is
target-rank sensitivity, not a width ablation.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Structural-Control Definitions}
\label{app:structural-control-definitions}

The sorted spanning-path control constructs a geometry-independent path through
the shuffled batch and matches the independently sorted student and teacher
edge lengths. The kNN control similarly matches independently sorted directed
nearest-neighbor distances with $k=1$. Teacher-MST first selects the teacher's
$B-1$ minimum-spanning-tree edges and then compares student distances on those
same sample pairs, retaining edge correspondence. Native Gram matching compares
all pairwise cosine similarities. Finally, $H_0$ matches independently sorted
student and teacher MST edge weights and discards edge correspondence. The
path, Teacher-MST, and $H_0$ controls expose $B-1$ scalar constraints per batch,
kNN exposes $B$, and Gram matching is deliberately denser.

\section{Representation Diagnostics}
\label{app:layerwise-cka}

We examine whether final-embedding supervision changes the internal
representations of the student even though GATE-KD applies no
intermediate-layer objective. The probe contains 512 unique sentences sampled
from the validation splits after excluding sentences found in the distillation
corpus. For every teacher and student Transformer layer, we compute centered
linear CKA between their pooled representations \citep{kornblith2019cka}.
Results for the trained students are averaged over seeds 42, 43, and 44. We
omit the embedding-output layer because special-token pooling makes this
representation constant across examples, for which centered CKA is undefined.

Figure~\ref{fig:layerwise-raw-cka} shows that the pretrained students already
share substantial representation structure with their teachers. Raw CKA alone
therefore combines correspondence inherited from pretraining with changes
induced by distillation.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/layerwise_raw_cka_all_pairs}
    \caption{Layerwise linear CKA for the pretrained student, GATE-KD, and
    TALAS across three configurations. Trained results are averaged over three
    seeds.}
    \label{fig:layerwise-raw-cka}
\end{figure}

To contextualize representation similarity relative to initialization,
Figure~\ref{fig:layerwise-delta-cka} subtracts the pretrained-student CKA matrix
from the mean matrix after distillation. GATE-KD produces higher
final-to-final CKA than TALAS in all
three configurations. The raw values are 0.8266, 0.8447, and 0.7845 for
GATE-KD, compared with 0.7741, 0.7901, and 0.7333 for TALAS in configurations
(a), (b), and (c), respectively. The corresponding changes from initialization
are 0.2992, 0.2773, and 0.2781 for GATE-KD, compared with 0.2468, 0.2227, and
0.2269 for TALAS. GATE-KD consequently exhibits consistently higher
final-to-final CKA across the three configurations, with differences ranging
from 0.051 to 0.055.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/layerwise_delta_cka_all_pairs}
    \caption{Change in layerwise linear CKA relative to the pretrained
    student. Red and blue denote increased and decreased similarity,
    respectively.}
    \label{fig:layerwise-delta-cka}
\end{figure}

The changes are distributed across multiple later student layers rather than
being confined to a single teacher--student layer pair. Thus final-embedding
supervision co-occurs with measurable changes in later student representations
despite the absence of explicit intermediate-layer matching. Some layer pairs
decrease in CKA, so the result does not imply uniformly stronger alignment at
every depth. CKA is descriptive: it does not establish that the observed
changes cause downstream improvements or that endpoint supervision
mechanistically substitutes for intermediate-layer distillation. It is also
invariant to orthogonal transformations and isotropic scaling, so higher CKA
measures representational similarity rather than transferred task
information. The figures report seed means only; we have not measured
seed-level variance of the CKA matrices, included a fixed-PCA control without
a fitted gauge, or tested whether per-layer CKA changes correlate with
downstream differences. We therefore use these probes to document that
endpoint supervision co-occurs with changes in later layers, and for nothing
stronger.
