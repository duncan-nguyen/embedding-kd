\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

GATE-KD exposes a hidden choice in cross-dimensional distillation: projection
selects what teacher variation remains, but not how its coordinates are
presented to the student. A corpus-level Procrustes fit resolves this ambiguity
before optimization, enabling direct supervision of the deployed embedding
without a trainable map or layer correspondence.

The compact controls provide the clearest evidence. The fixed endpoint
interface exceeds two learned linear maps by at least 4.95 points; one fitted
orientation is within 0.16 points of four refits; and aligned random subspaces
approach PCA despite much lower retained energy. Across three model pairs, the
full recipe has the highest numerical nine-task average, while endpoint
supervision supplies most of its gain and $H_0$ remains auxiliary.

The scope is deliberately narrower than universal embedding transfer. Results
use three seeds and task-adapted text; projector controls cover only two linear
maps; and the minibatch-invariant comparison also changes target stationarity
and fit rank. Proposition~\ref{prop:local-global-gap} explains the static
objective relaxation, not the downstream gap. Paired multi-seed analysis, a
stronger global-invariant control, general-text distillation, and end-to-end
timing remain the highest-priority tests.
