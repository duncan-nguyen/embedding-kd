\subsection{What Makes the Interface Work?}
\label{sec:analysis}

We use the compact configuration (c) to compare fixed versus learned
interfaces, persistent versus stepwise alignment, and fixed versus refreshed
targets. Additional controls appear in Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-analysis}.

\subsubsection{Does the Interface Need to Be Learned?}
\label{sec:analysis-interface}

All rows in Table~\ref{tab:interface-families} use endpoint-only supervision.
The learned controls train one bias-free linear map in either direction under
the same AdamW schedule; the fixed controls construct targets before training.

PCA--Procrustes reaches 74.28 Avg., versus 69.33 for the stronger learned map
and 69.35 for unaligned PCA. A fixed target is therefore not enough; its fitted
orientation matters. This result is limited to two bias-free linear maps and
does not rank stronger learned projector families.

\subsubsection{Persistent Corpus-Level Targets versus Stepwise Invariant Matching}
\label{sec:analysis-gauge}

Table~\ref{tab:gauge-mechanism} varies the orientation treatment under the full
objective. Its invariant row re-solves Procrustes in every minibatch; unlike the
fixed rows, it couples local estimation, a moving target, and potentially
rank-deficient cross-covariance. It is therefore an interface comparison, not a
single-factor test of locality.

\input{tables/gauge_mechanism}

Raw PCA, a random fixed orientation, and fitted $R^{(0)}$ score 72.51, 73.02,
and 74.63. Fitting the orientation therefore adds 1.61 points over a random
fixed gauge and 2.12 over raw coordinates. This supports checkpoint-conditioned
presentation, not information in the rotation itself.

The stepwise invariant control scores $72.74\pm0.07$, 1.89 below fixed
$R^{(0)}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:local-global-gap} characterizes its extra
batchwise freedom for fixed representations but does not predict this downstream
gap; moving targets and minibatch rank remain confounds. A memory-bank or
periodic full-corpus invariant control is needed to separate them.

One refit scores 74.64 and four refits 74.79, only 0.01 and 0.16 above
$R^{(0)}$. Representative selection matters more than repeated alignment here.
Aligned random subspaces also remain within 0.34 points of PCA
(Appendix~\ref{app:pca-controls}), so the evidence favors a stable,
student-aligned target over a particular high-energy subspace.
