\begin{abstract}
Embedding distillation transfers knowledge from a large teacher to a smaller student, yet their representations often differ in dimension and orientation. Existing methods handle this mismatch with learned projections, intermediate-layer supervision, or auxiliary objectives. We argue that the main challenge is making the final embedding spaces comparable. To address this, we propose GATE-KD, a final-embedding distillation framework that combines endpoint alignment with native-space topology transfer. A fixed principal-component projection first selects teacher information that fits the student dimension. A closed-form orthogonal alignment then follows the evolving student across epochs without learning extra parameters or altering the teacher structure. In parallel, a topology-based loss transfers the connectivity of examples directly between the original teacher and student spaces, despite their different dimensions. GATE-KD therefore supervises only final embeddings and adds no inference-time components. Across diverse teacher--student configurations and downstream tasks, it consistently matches or outperforms strong baselines. Further analyses show that repeated alignment improves learning even when the teacher geometry remains unchanged, while direct structure matching complements pointwise targets. Our results suggest that a carefully designed endpoint interface can replace more complex distillation pipelines.
\end{abstract}
