\begin{abstract}
Embedding distillation transfers knowledge from a large teacher to a smaller student, yet their representations often differ in dimension and orientation. Existing methods handle this mismatch with trainable projections, intermediate-layer supervision, or auxiliary objectives. We argue that a central challenge is making the final embedding spaces comparable. To address this, we propose GATE-KD, a final-embedding distillation framework that combines endpoint alignment with complementary native-space topology transfer. A fixed principal-component projection first selects teacher information that fits the student dimension. A closed-form orthogonal alignment then follows the evolving student across epochs without learning extra parameters or altering the teacher structure. In parallel, a topology-based loss transfers the connectivity of examples directly between the original teacher and student spaces, despite their different dimensions. GATE-KD therefore uses only cached final teacher embeddings and introduces no trainable alignment module. Across three teacher--student configurations and nine downstream benchmarks, GATE-KD obtains the highest aggregate score among the evaluated distilled students, improving over the next-best baseline by 0.38--1.59 points while reducing measured GPU optimization time by 62--74\%. Controlled analyses support the roles of a student-aligned endpoint interface and complementary native-space connectivity transfer. These results establish a carefully designed endpoint interface as a competitive alternative to more complex distillation pipelines.
\end{abstract}
