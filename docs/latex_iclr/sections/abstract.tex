\begin{abstract}
Distilling text embeddings across architectures involves two mismatches:
teacher and student may have different widths and different coordinate systems.
Projection resolves the first, but leaves the second: rotating every projected
target preserves all pairwise similarities while changing the gradients of a
pointwise loss. GATE-KD turns this ambiguity into an interface. It projects
cached teacher endpoints, uses a corpus-level Procrustes fit to express them in
coordinates aligned with the pretrained student, and supervises the student's
deployed embedding directly, without a learned cross-space map or teacher hidden
states. Under a label-free, task-adapted protocol, the full recipe obtains the
highest numerical average in three teacher--student configurations, with the
clearest gain for a 22M student (+1.59 points). In a matched endpoint-only test,
the fixed interface scores 74.28, versus 69.35 without alignment and 69.33 for
the stronger learned linear map. Re-estimating the alignment every epoch adds
only 0.16 points, while aligned random subspaces remain within 0.34 points of
PCA despite retaining far less teacher energy. These results support
student-aligned target presentation as the core design choice in the evaluated
regime; an optional native-space $H_0$ term provides a smaller auxiliary gain.
\end{abstract}
