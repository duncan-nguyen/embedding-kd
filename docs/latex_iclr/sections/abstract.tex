\begin{abstract}
Embedding distillation across different teacher and student dimensions is commonly handled with learned cross-space projectors, intermediate-layer alignment, or relational objectives. These approaches either optimize through an auxiliary interface that does not uniquely constrain the geometry of the deployed student embedding, require additional alignment machinery and correspondence choices, or discard concrete coordinate supervision altogether. We instead view target construction as two separate decisions: reducing the teacher representation to the student dimension and choosing how that representation should be expressed to the pretrained student. GATE-KD makes this separation explicit. It constructs a student-width teacher representation and selects a student-compatible coordinate realization in closed form, directly supervising the deployed embedding without a trainable cross-space interface or intermediate-layer correspondence. The distinction is substantial: with retained teacher information and relational geometry held fixed, orthogonally equivalent targets differ by 4.93 points across nine benchmarks. Across three teacher–student configurations, two distillation corpora, and zero-shot retrieval, GATE-KD achieves the highest average in eight of nine evaluated settings, improves over the strongest baseline by up to 1.59 points, and reduces measured optimization-loop time by up to 4.0×. GATE-KD thus achieves stronger embedding distillation with less alignment machinery.
\end{abstract}
