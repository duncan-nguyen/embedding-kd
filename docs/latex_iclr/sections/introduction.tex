\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Text embeddings support retrieval, similarity, clustering, and classification,
but large encoders can be costly to deploy. Knowledge distillation transfers
their behavior to compact students using unlabeled text
\citep{hinton2015distilling,reimers2020multilingual}. Unlike class logits,
embeddings from independently pretrained models need not share a width or a
coordinate system. Prior methods bridge this mismatch with learned projectors,
relational losses, or selected hidden layers
\citep{park2019rkd,tian2020crd,xu2023distillcse,zhang2024jasper,truong2025emo,talas}.
We ask whether final teacher endpoints alone can provide a direct target for the
embedding that the student will deploy.

Dimensional reduction and coordinate alignment solve different problems.
Projection chooses which teacher variation survives at student width; the
remaining axes can still be rotated. Imagine a map on a transparent sheet:
rotating it changes no distance between cities, but changes every $(x,y)$
coordinate. Likewise, an orthogonal rotation preserves all target similarities
yet changes the gradients of pointwise distillation. We call choosing one such
student-relative orientation \emph{gauge fixing}. It selects a presentation for
optimization, not canonical teacher coordinates.

This view leads to \textbf{GATE-KD}, Gauge-Aligned Target Embeddings for
Knowledge Distillation. GATE-KD caches final teacher embeddings, projects them
to student width, and uses closed-form orthogonal Procrustes alignment to orient
the targets toward the pretrained student. Only the student is trained; the
target map is fixed within each epoch and absent at inference. Procrustes is
classical---the design choice is to apply it on the target side instead of
learning a projector or re-solving the orientation inside every minibatch loss.
An optional native-space $H_0$ term transfers coarse connectivity scales.

Across three teacher--student pairs and nine sentence benchmarks, GATE-KD has
the highest numerical average under a label-free, task-adapted protocol. The
clearest margin is 1.59 points for the 22M student; the 0.38 and 0.48 margins for
larger students are descriptive three-seed comparisons. In compact matched
controls, the endpoint-only fixed interface exceeds the stronger of two learned
linear maps by 4.95 points. A fitted initial orientation is within 0.16 points
of epoch-wise refitting, and aligned random subspaces are within 0.34 points of
PCA despite much lower retained energy. Thus the main empirical story is the
student-aligned presentation of a fixed endpoint target, rather than frequent
alignment or PCA-specific variance retention.

We make three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We separate cross-dimensional endpoint distillation into subspace
    selection and student-relative target orientation.
    \item We introduce a closed-form target-side alignment for direct endpoint
    supervision, with no trainable cross-space map or layer correspondence.
    \item We evaluate three model pairs and matched compact-student controls
    that isolate target construction, orientation, and refit frequency.
\end{itemize}
