\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Dense text embeddings are a common interface between language models and
applications such as semantic search, retrieval-augmented generation,
clustering, classification, and semantic textual similarity. Strong embedding
models increasingly rely on large encoders, broad training corpora, and high
dimensional representations, all of which raise inference and deployment
costs. Knowledge distillation offers a practical alternative: an expensive
teacher can supervise a compact encoder that retains much of its semantic
quality while remaining inexpensive to serve
\citep{hinton2015distilling,reimers2020multilingual}.

Most embedding distillation methods transfer \emph{representation states}.
They align the final student embedding with the teacher, add contrastive or
relational constraints, or extend the same supervision to selected student
layers \citep{xu2023distillcse,zhang2024jasper,truong2025emo}. Recent
depth-aware approaches further encourage high-level semantic information to
appear in shallower representations or anchor several upper student layers to
a final teacher embedding \citep{li2025ese,talas}. Despite their differences,
these objectives primarily constrain a collection of states
$\{\mathbf{z}^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}$. They do not explicitly describe the
transition by which one level of representation should become the next.

This distinction matters when teacher and student have substantially different
capacities. A shallow Transformer layer must preserve information useful for
subsequent computation, whereas a deep teacher embedding already represents a
terminal semantic abstraction. Requiring many shallow or intermediate layers
to imitate that same endpoint can therefore conflict with the student's native
hierarchy. More layer-wise supervision is not necessarily more informative: it
may simply ask the student to arrive at the answer prematurely. This motivates
our central question:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Can a teacher specify how student representations should move through
depth, rather than only where they should end?}
\end{quote}

The continuous-depth view of residual networks provides a useful language for
answering this question. Residual transformations can be interpreted as
discrete approximations to continuous dynamics, an idea underlying Neural ODEs
and ODE-inspired Transformers \citep{chen2018neuralode,li2022odetransformer}.
We use this perspective as a supervision principle, not as an architectural
claim. We neither replace Transformer blocks with a Neural ODE nor learn an
auxiliary flow that post-processes the student output. Instead, the teacher
defines an analytic semantic vector field, and the student's existing layers
are trained to realize its local transitions.

We propose \textbf{GeoODE-KD}, a teacher-guided geometric dynamics framework
for sentence embedding distillation. Because sentence embeddings are commonly
normalized and compared by cosine similarity, we model them on the unit
hypersphere. Cached teacher outputs define a semantic potential with two
components: an instance term that attracts each student sentence toward its
teacher target and a relational term that transfers the teacher's native
pairwise cosine geometry. The negative Riemannian gradient of this potential
provides a tangent vector that points toward lower teacher discrepancy. We run
this field under a finite-horizon time reparameterization so that the amount of
semantic displacement remaining is explicitly tied to the amount of student
depth remaining.

GeoODE-KD then assigns each student layer a normalized depth coordinate. From a
representation at depth $t_\ell$, the local vector field and an exponential-map
update predict the representation expected at $t_{\ell+1}$. A dynamics loss
aligns the actual next Transformer layer with this prediction, while endpoint
and contrastive terms supervise the final embedding. Thus, intermediate layers
are not independently pulled toward a common terminal state; each is trained to
perform a prescribed fraction of the remaining semantic transformation.

This formulation separates training-time guidance from inference-time
architecture. The teacher is evaluated once, and only its final sentence
embeddings are cached. GeoODE-KD uses no teacher hidden states, attention maps,
learned projection head, continuous solver, or auxiliary flow network during
student optimization. All geometric operations are discarded after training,
so inference uses the unmodified student encoder. The formulation also yields
testable depth-wise predictions: the ideal flow preserves hyperspherical norms
and decreases its teacher-conditioned energy monotonically; without the
relational component, geodesic distance to the teacher follows a closed-form
depth profile and reaches zero at the endpoint.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate sentence embedding distillation as supervision of a
    \emph{trajectory} through representation depth, shifting the target of
    transfer from isolated states to transitions between consecutive layers.

    \item We derive a teacher-conditioned Riemannian vector field that combines
    point-wise semantic alignment with native teacher geometry, together with a
    finite-horizon parameterization that yields explicit, depth-dependent local
    targets without a learned ODE module.

    \item We train the existing Transformer layers as a discrete realization of
    this flow using a dynamics-consistency objective. The method requires only
    cached teacher outputs, adds no inference-time parameters, and makes its
    trajectory hypothesis directly measurable at every student depth.
\end{itemize}
