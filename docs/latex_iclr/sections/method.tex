\section{GeoODE-KD: From Teacher Embeddings to Semantic Dynamics}
\label{sec:geoode}

Given an unlabeled corpus $\mathcal{D}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$, a teacher $f_T$,
and a student $f_S$ with $L$ Transformer layers, our goal is to train the
student without labels or online teacher inference. GeoODE-KD follows four
steps. It first caches and maps teacher embeddings into the student space. It
then converts teacher discrepancy into a tangent vector field, assigns this
field a finite depth horizon, and uses the resulting local updates to supervise
consecutive student layers. All four steps define training targets; none adds a
module to the student.

\subsection{Teacher Target Construction}
\label{sec:teacher-space}

Let
\begin{equation}
    f_T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_T},
    \qquad
    f_S:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_S}
\end{equation}
denote the teacher and student embedding functions. The offline stage runs
$f_T$ once and caches
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_i
    =
    \operatorname{norm}\!\left(f_T(x_i)\right),
    \qquad
    \operatorname{norm}(\mathbf{x})
    =
    \frac{\mathbf{x}}{\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_2+\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:native-teacher}
\end{equation}
No teacher hidden state or attention map is retained.

Point-wise supervision requires both models to use the same width. For
$d_T\geq d_S$, we fit a fixed PCA basis
$P_{\mathrm{PCA}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_T\times d_S}$ on the cached training
embeddings. This basis selects a maximum-variance $d_S$-dimensional teacher
subspace, but its orientation is arbitrary. The orientation leaves cosine
geometry unchanged, yet a point-wise loss would still force the pretrained
student to match it.

We fix this coordinate \emph{gauge} before training. Let
$\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{PCA}}$ contain normalized PCA coordinates for a fitting
subset, and let $\mathbf{Z}^{(L)}_0$ contain normalized output embeddings of the
initial student for the same sentences. We solve orthogonal Procrustes
\citep{schonemann1966procrustes}:
\begin{equation}
    R^\star
    =
    \underset{R\in O(d_S)}{\arg\min}\,
    \left\lVert
        \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{PCA}}R-\mathbf{Z}^{(L)}_0
    \right\rVert_F,
    \qquad
    R^\star=UV^\top,
    \quad
    U\Sigma V^\top
    =
    \operatorname{svd}\!\left(
        \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{PCA}}^\top\mathbf{Z}^{(L)}_0
    \right).
    \label{eq:gauge-alignment}
\end{equation}
The shared-space point-wise target is then
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}_i
    =
    \operatorname{norm}\!\left(
        f_T(x_i)P_{\mathrm{PCA}}R^\star
    \right)
    \in\mathbb{S}^{d_S-1}.
    \label{eq:projected-teacher}
\end{equation}
When $d_T=d_S$, $P_{\mathrm{PCA}}$ can be the identity. The rotation
$R^\star$ changes neither pairwise cosines nor retained variance. We freeze both
maps after fitting; Appendix~\ref{app:target-construction} gives the target
construction details.

For the student, let $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_i$ be the token states after
Transformer layer $\ell$. Pooling and normalization produce
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}^{(\ell)}_i
    =
    \operatorname{norm}\!\left(
        \operatorname{Pool}(\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_i)
    \right)
    \in\mathbb{S}^{d_S-1}.
    \label{eq:student-layer-embedding}
\end{equation}
For a mini-batch of $B$ sentences, we stack these row-wise into
$\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell)},\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times d_S}$.

Point-wise alignment uses the projected targets $\mathbf{T}$, but pairwise
cosines do not require equal widths. For each mini-batch, we construct the
native teacher Gram matrix
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{G}_T
    =
    \widetilde{\mathbf{T}}\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}^\top,
    \label{eq:native-teacher-gram}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}$ stacks the embeddings in
Eq.~\ref{eq:native-teacher}. This matrix is independent of teacher width and
invariant to $R^\star$, so projection affects only the point-wise target.

\subsection{Teacher-Conditioned Vector Field}
\label{sec:vector-field}

The next stage turns teacher discrepancy into a direction of motion. For a
student state $\mathbf{z}_i$ and projected target $\boldsymbol{\tau}_i$, the
hyperspherical distance is
\begin{equation}
    d_g(\mathbf{z}_i,\boldsymbol{\tau}_i)
    =
    \arccos(\mathbf{z}_i^\top\boldsymbol{\tau}_i).
\end{equation}
The point-wise potential averages its squared value,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{T})
    =
    \frac{1}{2B}
    \sum_{i=1}^{B}
    d_g^2(\mathbf{z}_i,\boldsymbol{\tau}_i).
    \label{eq:semantic-potential}
\end{equation}
Its negative Riemannian gradient points along the shortest geodesic to the
teacher target.

The relational potential compares the student Gram matrix with the native
teacher matrix from Eq.~\ref{eq:native-teacher-gram}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{geo}}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{G}_T)
    =
    \frac{1}{B^2}
    \left\lVert
        \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^\top-\mathbf{G}_T
    \right\rVert_F^2.
    \label{eq:geometric-potential}
\end{equation}
We combine the two terms as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{Z};\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G}_T)
    =
    \alpha\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{T})
    +
    \beta\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{geo}}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{G}_T)
    .
    \label{eq:teacher-potential}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha,\beta\geq0$. GeoODE-KD does not minimize this expression
separately at every layer. It differentiates the potential to obtain the next
transition target.

For $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{S}^{d_S-1}$, the projection of an ambient vector
$\mathbf{u}$ onto the tangent space is
\begin{equation}
    \Pi_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{u})
    =
    \mathbf{u}-(\mathbf{z}^\top\mathbf{u})\mathbf{z}.
    \label{eq:tangent-projection}
\end{equation}
The hyperspherical logarithmic map is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Log}_{\mathbf{z}}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
    =
    \frac{\theta}{\sin\theta}
    \Pi_{\mathbf{z}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}),
    \qquad
    \theta=d_g(\mathbf{z},\boldsymbol{\tau}).
    \label{eq:sphere-log-map}
\end{equation}
Applying both maps row-wise gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G}_T)
    =
    \alpha\operatorname{Log}_{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{T})
    -
    \frac{4\beta}{B}
    \Pi_{\mathbf{Z}}
    \!\left[
        (\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^\top-\mathbf{G}_T)\mathbf{Z}
    \right]
    .
    \label{eq:semantic-vector-direction}
\end{equation}
This direction equals $-B\operatorname{grad}_{\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{E}$. The
factor $B$ removes the scale introduced by batch averaging, so flow speed does
not change with mini-batch size.

\subsection{Finite-Horizon Depth Schedule}
\label{sec:finite-horizon}

A standard gradient flow approaches its target asymptotically, while the
student has only $L$ layers. We map these layers to $t\in[0,1]$. Let
$s(t)\geq0$ allocate guidance over depth, with remaining mass
\begin{equation}
    R(t)=\int_t^1 s(u)\,du.
    \label{eq:remaining-guidance}
\end{equation}
The depth-indexed field is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\mathbf{Z}(t)}{dt}
    =
    \frac{s(t)}{R(t)}
    \mathbf{V}\!\left(
        \mathbf{Z}(t),\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G}_T
    \right)
    ,
    \label{eq:finite-horizon-flow}
\end{equation}
for $t<1$. The ratio $s(t)/R(t)$ maps unbounded gradient-flow time into this
finite interval. We use $s(t)=t$, which gives $R(t)=(1-t^2)/2$ and places more
guidance near the output. Constant and power schedules are direct alternatives.

The instance-only case shows what this schedule controls. For $\alpha=1$ and
$\beta=0$, let $\theta(t)=d_g(\mathbf{z}(t),\boldsymbol{\tau})$. The log map has
norm $\theta(t)$ and points along the target geodesic, so
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\theta}(t)
    =
    -\frac{s(t)}{R(t)}\theta(t),
    \qquad
    \theta(t)=\theta(0)\frac{R(t)}{R(0)}
    .
    \label{eq:closed-form-contraction}
\end{equation}
With $s(t)=t$, this becomes $\theta(t)=\theta(0)(1-t^2)$ and
$\theta(1)=0$. Exact arrival applies to the instance-only field. When
$\beta>0$, the relational term couples examples and may bend individual paths;
the joint potential still decreases under the ideal continuous dynamics
(Section~\ref{sec:objective-properties}).

\subsection{Layer-Wise Flow Supervision}
\label{sec:flow-integrator}

During training, the $L$ Transformer layers provide states at
$t_\ell=\ell/L$; no external solver evaluates
Eq.~\ref{eq:finite-horizon-flow}. The fraction of remaining point-wise
displacement assigned to transition $\ell$ is
\begin{align}
    \rho_\ell
    &=
    1-
    \exp\!\left(
        -\int_{t_\ell}^{t_{\ell+1}}
        \frac{s(u)}{R(u)}\,du
    \right)
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    1-
    \frac{R(t_{\ell+1})}{R(t_\ell)}
    \in(0,1].
    \label{eq:finite-step-fraction}
\end{align}
This integrated form remains well-defined on the final interval and gives
$\rho_{L-1}=1$ for any valid schedule.

At layer $\ell$, we evaluate the field once and construct the next target as
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{(\ell+1)}
    =
    \operatorname{Exp}_{\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell)}}
    \!\left(
        \rho_\ell
        \mathbf{V}(\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell)},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G}_T)
    \right).
    \label{eq:flow-predicted-state}
\end{equation}
For tangent vector $\mathbf{v}$, the spherical exponential map is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Exp}_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{v})
    =
    \cos(\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert_2)\mathbf{z}
    +
    \sin(\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert_2)
    \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert_2},
    \label{eq:sphere-exp-map}
\end{equation}
with its continuous extension at $\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}$. This update remains
on the hypersphere for any local step size. For $\beta=0$ and $\alpha=1$, it is
spherical interpolation toward the teacher by fraction $\rho_\ell$. With the
relational term, it is a one-step discretization of the coupled field, not an
exact solution of the nonlinear trajectory.

The Transformer produces the actual state $\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell+1)}$. On the
hypersphere, the actual movement made by the student from layer $\ell$ to
$\ell+1$ is the tangent vector
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{U}^{(\ell)}
    =
    \operatorname{Log}_{\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell)}}
    \!\left(\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell+1)}\right),
    \label{eq:actual-velocity}
\end{equation}
and the desired teacher-conditioned vector field at the same state is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}^{(\ell)}
    =
    \mathbf{V}\!\left(\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell)},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G}_T\right)
    =
    -B\operatorname{grad}_{\mathbb{S}}
    \mathcal{E}\!\left(\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell)};\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G}_T\right).
    \label{eq:desired-velocity}
\end{equation}
The velocity loss asks the realized transition to point along the field:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}
    =
    \frac{1}{L-1}
    \sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}
    \frac{1}{B}
    \sum_{i=1}^{B}
    \left[
        1-\cos\!\left(
            \mathbf{u}^{(\ell)}_i,
            \operatorname{sg}\!\left[\mathbf{v}^{(\ell)}_i\right]
        \right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:velocity-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{u}^{(\ell)}_i$ and $\mathbf{v}^{(\ell)}_i$ are the rows of
$\mathbf{U}^{(\ell)}$ and $\mathbf{V}^{(\ell)}$, $\cos(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the
cosine between tangent vectors, and $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients through
the field. Both vectors live in the tangent space of $\mathbf{Z}^{(\ell)}$, so
the comparison is well defined. The cosine is scale-free: the positive warp
$s(t)/R(t)$ and the step fraction $\rho_\ell$ change the length of the
prescribed step but not its direction, so $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}$ trains
\emph{where} each layer moves and leaves \emph{how far} to the Transformer.
Stop-gradient makes the field a fixed local target for the current forward
pass, so the loss cannot be lowered by moving the source state to reorient the
field. Every transition is covered, including the last one $\ell=L-1$ into the
final layer: the endpoint objective below fixes \emph{where} that layer must
land, while $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}$ asks that it be reached along the
field rather than by an arbitrary detour.

\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:objective-properties}

The final layer must still satisfy the endpoint condition. We use
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{end}}
    =
    \frac{1}{B}
    \sum_{i=1}^{B}
    \left(
        1-(\mathbf{z}^{(L)}_i)^\top\boldsymbol{\tau}_i
    \right).
    \label{eq:endpoint-objective}
\end{equation}
No earlier layer receives this direct target. In the instance-only case, the
field at $\mathbf{Z}^{(L-1)}$ points along the geodesic to
$\boldsymbol{\tau}_i$ and $\rho_{L-1}=1$ places its exact step on the teacher,
so $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{end}}$ is the boundary condition of the same path:
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}$ orients the final transition and
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{end}}$ fixes where it must stop.
With relational guidance, it remains an explicit endpoint anchor.

We also apply unsupervised contrastive learning at the output. For two dropout
views $\mathbf{z}^{(L,a)}_i$ and $\mathbf{z}^{(L,b)}_i$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ctr}}
    =
    -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}
    \log
    \frac{
        \exp((\mathbf{z}^{(L,a)}_i)^\top
        \mathbf{z}^{(L,b)}_i/\tau_c)
    }{
        \sum_{j=1}^{B}
        \exp((\mathbf{z}^{(L,a)}_i)^\top
        \mathbf{z}^{(L,b)}_j/\tau_c)
    }.
    \label{eq:contrastive-objective}
\end{equation}
Restricting this term to the output avoids a second layer-wise objective. The
training loss is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}
    =
    \lambda_{\mathrm{end}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{end}}
    +
    \lambda_{\mathrm{vel}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}
    +
    \lambda_{\mathrm{ctr}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ctr}}
    .
    \label{eq:total-training-objective}
\end{equation}
Here, $\lambda_{\mathrm{end}}$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{vel}}$, and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{ctr}}$ weight endpoint, transition, and contrastive
supervision. The potential $\mathcal{E}$ generates a direction; it is not an
additional layer-wise loss. The term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}$ measures
whether the Transformer follows that direction.

\paragraph{Properties of the ideal flow.}
The construction gives three properties. First, the field is tangent to the
product of hyperspheres. Consequently,
$d\lVert\mathbf{z}(t)\rVert_2^2/dt=2\mathbf{z}(t)^\top
\dot{\mathbf{z}}(t)=0$, so continuous trajectories preserve embedding norms.
Second, for $t<1$ the teacher-conditioned potential decreases monotonically:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dt}
    =
    -\frac{B\,s(t)}{R(t)}
    \left\lVert
        \operatorname{grad}_{\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{E}
    \right\rVert_F^2
    \leq0.
    \label{eq:energy-descent}
\end{equation}
Third, Eq.~\ref{eq:closed-form-contraction} gives exact geodesic contraction for
the instance-only field. These are properties of the ideal continuous flow;
whether finite Transformer layers realize them is measured empirically. Full
derivations are given in Appendix~\ref{app:derivations}.

\paragraph{Training and inference.}
Across $L$ layers, the Gram term costs $\mathcal{O}(LB^2d_S)$ training
operations and $\mathcal{O}(B^2)$ temporary memory, independent of teacher
size. Target construction and geometric supervision are removed at deployment,
so inference uses the original student architecture and parameter count.
Implementation details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.
