\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

Let $f_T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_T}$ be a frozen teacher and
$f_S(\cdot;\theta):\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_S}$ a pretrained
student. Given an unlabeled distillation corpus
$\mathcal{C}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$, we denote their normalized embeddings by
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{t}_i=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(f_T(x_i)\right),
    \qquad
    \mathbf{z}_i(\theta)=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(f_S(x_i;\theta)\right),
    \label{eq:normalized-embeddings}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}_i$ is the student embedding used directly at inference.

When $d_T\neq d_S$, embedding distillation must resolve two separate choices.
The first is \emph{dimension reduction}: which teacher representation should be
retained at width $d_S$. The second is \emph{coordinate selection}: once that
representation has been reduced, which coordinate realization should be
presented to the pretrained student. GATE-KD treats these choices explicitly
rather than learning a cross-space interface that couples them.

\subsection{Dimension Reduction Does Not Determine a Distillation Target}
\label{sec:dimension-reduction}

We first construct a fixed teacher representation at the student width. In our
implementation, when $d_T>d_S$, we fit the leading $d_S$ PCA directions
$P\in\mathbb{R}^{d_T\times d_S}$ on the centered teacher cache and define
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{y}_i=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(\mathbf{t}_iP\right).
    \label{eq:reduced-target}
\end{equation}
Centering is used only to estimate the PCA directions: the mean is not
subtracted from $\mathbf{t}_iP$, and the retained components are not whitened.
PCA is only our default dimension-reduction operator. GATE-KD applies to any
fixed procedure that produces a student-width teacher matrix
$Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_S}$.

Crucially, constructing $Y$ does not finish target construction. For every
orthogonal matrix $R\in O(d_S)$,
\begin{equation}
    (YR)(YR)^\top=YY^\top.
    \label{eq:gram-invariance}
\end{equation}
Thus,
\begin{equation}
    [Y]_{O(d_S)}=\{YR:R\in O(d_S)\}
    \label{eq:target-orbit}
\end{equation}
contains an entire family of reduced teacher representations with identical
pairwise inner products and Euclidean distances. Dimension reduction determines
the relational geometry to retain, but not the concrete coordinates in which
that geometry is presented to the student.

This remaining choice matters whenever distillation directly supervises the
student endpoint. For a fixed student matrix
$Z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_S}$, consider the mean cosine loss toward $YR$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathcal{L}_{Z}(R)
        &=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
          \left(1-\langle\mathbf{z}_i,\mathbf{y}_iR\rangle\right) \\
        &=1-\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(R^\top Y^\top Z\right).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:orientation-loss}
\end{equation}
Although every $YR$ has the same relational geometry, its loss against the
pretrained student depends on $R$. In particular,
\begin{equation}
    \min_{R\in O(d_S)}\mathcal{L}_{Z}(R)
    =1-\frac{\lVert Y^\top Z\rVert_*}{N},
    \label{eq:orientation-optimum}
\end{equation}
where $\lVert\cdot\rVert_*$ denotes the nuclear norm. Hence geometric
equivalence after dimension reduction does not imply equivalence as an
optimization target. The remaining problem is to choose a coordinate
realization of $Y$ relative to the student that will actually be optimized.

\subsection{Student-Conditioned Coordinate Selection}
\label{sec:coordinate-selection}

GATE-KD makes coordinate selection an explicit second stage. Given the reduced
teacher representation $Y$ and the current student state $Z$, we choose
\begin{equation}
    R^\star
    =\underset{R\in O(d_S)}{\arg\min}\,
      \lVert YR-Z\rVert_F^2.
    \label{eq:procrustes-objective}
\end{equation}
Because the rows of $Y$ and $Z$ are normalized, this objective is equivalent to
minimizing the aggregate cosine endpoint loss in
Equation~\ref{eq:orientation-loss}. Given
\begin{equation}
    Y^\top Z=U\Sigma V^\top,
    \label{eq:procrustes-svd}
\end{equation}
the solution is available in closed form
\citep{schonemann1966procrustes}:
\begin{equation}
    R^\star=UV^\top,
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol{\tau}_i=\mathbf{y}_iR^\star.
    \label{eq:selected-target}
\end{equation}
The transformation changes only the coordinates of the reduced representation:
$YR^\star$ preserves exactly the same Gram matrix as $Y$. Its role is therefore
not to retain additional teacher information, but to choose how the retained
information is presented to this particular pretrained student. The selected
coordinates are student-dependent rather than canonical teacher coordinates.

We estimate this transformation at the \emph{corpus level} rather than
independently for each minibatch. Let
$\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{C}$ denote a representative fit set. At refresh
step $r$, we obtain normalized student embeddings $Z_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}$ in
evaluation mode and without gradients, and compute
\begin{equation}
    Y_{\mathcal{G}}^\top Z_{\mathcal{G}}^{(r)}
    =U\Sigma V^\top,
    \qquad
    R^{(r)}=UV^\top.
    \label{eq:coordinate-refresh}
\end{equation}
The same $R^{(r)}$ is then used for every minibatch until the next refresh,
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}_i^{(r)}=\mathbf{y}_iR^{(r)}.
    \label{eq:refreshed-target}
\end{equation}
For a minibatch $\mathcal{B}$, the endpoint objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{end}}^{(r)}
    =\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{B}\rvert}
      \sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}}
      \left(1-\left\langle
      \mathbf{z}_i,\boldsymbol{\tau}_i^{(r)}
      \right\rangle\right).
    \label{eq:endpoint-loss}
\end{equation}

A single corpus-level transformation is important because independently
choosing $R$ for different minibatches preserves geometry only within each
minibatch; their relative transformations need not define any single
representation of the full teacher corpus. A shared $R^{(r)}$, in contrast,
preserves the complete teacher Gram matrix while providing one persistent
endpoint target.

The representative fitting step is outside backpropagation. No gradients pass
through the student embeddings used to estimate $R^{(r)}$, the SVD, or the
cached teacher targets. Our default recipe selects $R^{(0)}$ from the pretrained
student and refreshes it after each non-final epoch. Ordinary student updates
therefore optimize directly against a fixed target between refreshes, without
introducing a trainable teacher-to-student projector or modifying the embedding
used at inference.

\subsection{Coordinate-Invariant Structural Support}
\label{sec:structural-support}

Coordinate selection determines the sample-specific endpoint target, but it
does not add relational information beyond that already contained in the
reduced teacher representation. We therefore complement the endpoint objective
with a structural signal computed directly in the native teacher and student
spaces. The auxiliary term is required to be invariant to orthogonal
transformations, so that it constrains geometry without introducing a second,
competing coordinate preference.

For normalized embeddings, we use chord distance
\begin{equation}
    d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
    =\sqrt{2-2\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle}.
    \label{eq:chord-distance}
\end{equation}
Within each minibatch of size $B$, we independently construct minimum spanning
trees over the native teacher embeddings $\{\mathbf{t}_i\}$ and current student
embeddings $\{\mathbf{z}_i\}$. The $B-1$ finite $H_0$ persistence death scales
coincide with the MST edge weights \citep{hofer2020topologically}. Let
\begin{equation}
    q_{(1)}^T\leq\cdots\leq q_{(B-1)}^T,
    \qquad
    q_{(1)}^S\leq\cdots\leq q_{(B-1)}^S
    \label{eq:sorted-mst-weights}
\end{equation}
be the sorted teacher and student edge weights. We define
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{H_0}
    =\frac{1}{B-1}\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}
      \left(q_{(j)}^S-q_{(j)}^T\right)^2.
    \label{eq:h0-loss}
\end{equation}
The two trees are constructed independently in their native spaces, so this
term requires neither equal embedding widths nor shared coordinates. Because
orthogonal transformations preserve inner products, they also preserve chord
distances, MSTs, and their edge weights. Consequently, $\mathcal{L}_{H_0}$ is
invariant to the same coordinate choice resolved by the endpoint term.

The two objectives therefore have separate roles.
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{end}}$ selects and supervises a concrete coordinate
realization relative to the student, whereas $\mathcal{L}_{H_0}$ constrains
native-space relational structure without preferring any realization. The
complete objective is
\begin{equation}
    {
        \mathcal{L}
        =\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{end}}
        +\lambda_{H_0}\mathcal{L}_{H_0}
    }
    \label{eq:complete-objective}
\end{equation}

Teacher embeddings and the dimension-reduction operator are computed once.
Each coordinate refresh requires only a no-gradient student pass over the fit
set and one $d_S\times d_S$ SVD; ordinary minibatch updates require no online
teacher forward pass. All target construction and structural computation are
removed after training, leaving only the student encoder and its normalized
endpoint at inference.
